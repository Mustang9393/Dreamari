# The First Airline Shift

Career: Airline Maintenance Technician. Level 1 role: Aircraft Support Mechanic.

Fictional workplace: Gannet Regional Airlines. The learner observes and drafts notes under Sora. Authorized staff retain technical decisions and aircraft release.

## Playable script and answer key

### 1. Before the next flight

**Control:** card

Your first week at fictional Gannet Regional Airlines starts in its coastal maintenance hangar.


### 2. The next team needs facts

**Control:** card

A passenger aircraft needs review. Prepare a finding brief: a short note listing concerns and unfinished work.


### 3. Sora has your first assignment

**Control:** card

“I’m Sora, your mechanic mentor. You’ll help me observe and record concerns. I’ll review your work.”


### 4. Learn under supervision

**Control:** card

“Use our current instructions for this aircraft and task. Trained, authorized staff decide when it can fly again.”


### 5. Caleb is setting up

**Control:** card

Caleb, another support mechanic, brings the check packet. He hopes to earn the next training assignment.


### 6. Sora asks you to help with a visual check. Which instructions should you open first?

**Control:** options

- Other: A procedure remembered from another aircraft — A familiar procedure may not apply to this aircraft.
- Correct: Current airline instructions for this aircraft and task — The reference must match the aircraft and the assigned work.
- Other: An online clip showing a similar check — A similar-looking check does not establish the applicable procedure.

### 7. Four words on the floor

**Control:** flips

- **Reported concern:** Something that needs checking. Example: a rub mark on a cable cover.
- **Part history:** Records linking a part to its source. Example: a supplier record.
- **Loose debris:** An item out of place that could cause harm. Example: a metal clipping.
- **Handoff:** Passing work and its status to another person. Example: naming an unfinished check.


### 8. Match each work situation to the term.

**Control:** match (unscored practice)

- A rub mark needs assessment → Reported concern
- A part’s paperwork links it to its source → Part history
- A loose metal clipping near the aircraft → Loose debris
- The next shift receives the unfinished check → Handoff


### 9. Check four situations from the hangar.

**Control:** rapid


A reported concern needs checking. A cable cover has a rub mark. Which note records what you saw?
- Correct: Rub mark observed; assessment needed — That states an observation without inventing a diagnosis.
- Other: Electrical system has failed — A rub mark alone does not prove a system failure.

Part history links a part to its source. The label and paperwork differ. What should you do?
- Other: Assume a similar appearance is enough — Appearance does not resolve a documentation mismatch.
- Correct: Ask the mentor to resolve the mismatch — The mismatch needs review before the part is used.

Loose debris can cause harm. Which item should you flag?
- Correct: A metal clipping on the work-area floor — Loose material can become foreign object debris.
- Other: A closed, secured storage case — The secured case is in its assigned place.

A handoff shares work status. One check is unfinished. What should the next shift hear?
- Other: All checks are complete — That hides unfinished work.
- Correct: Which check remains open and who reviews it next — The next team needs an accurate starting point.

### 10. Observe. Record. Flag.

**Control:** card

“Describe what you see and where. Flag concerns for review. Don’t guess the cause or choose a repair.”


### 11. During Sora’s check, you see a rub mark on the left cable cover. Its cause is unknown. What do you record?

**Control:** options

- Other: A confirmed electrical failure — You have not established an electrical failure.
- Other: Nothing, because no warning appeared — A visible concern still needs review even without a warning.
- Correct: Rub mark on left cable cover; ask Sora to review — The note gives the concern and location without guessing its cause.

### 12. Elena checks the evidence

**Control:** card

Elena, the maintenance supervisor, reviews your draft note and a replacement kit’s paperwork.


### 13. Looks are not enough

**Control:** card

“Compare the kit label with its records. If they differ, ask for review. Don’t guess why they differ.”


### 14. Keep your authority clear

**Control:** card

“Flag concerns for Sora. With training, you can become a technician, then a lead. Approval needs the right authority.”


### 15. The kit label says K-42; its paperwork says K-24. Gannet rules say hold mismatched kits for review. Choose two actions.

**Control:** pick

- Leave: Change the paperwork to match the kit
- Select: Put the kit in the marked hold area
- Leave: Claim the supplier sent a fake part
- Select: Tell Sora: label K-42, paperwork K-24
- Leave: Install it because the shape looks right

### 16. 

**Control:** card

Midpoint threshold: 68. Continue is available on either result.

### 17. The brief moves forward

**Control:** card

Your draft is ready. The kit stays on hold while authorized staff review the mismatch.


### 18. Caleb wants to be noticed

**Control:** card

Caleb returns from setup. He hopes today’s effort will earn him another supervised assignment.


### 19. Your work. His claim.

**Control:** card

Caleb calls your finding brief his own. The saved draft history shows your work.


### 20. Caleb claims your finding brief. The draft history shows you prepared it. How should you raise the issue?

**Control:** options

- Other: Accuse Caleb over the hangar radio — A public accusation interrupts work and escalates the conflict.
- Correct: Speak privately with Sora and show the draft history — A calm private conversation gives the mentor facts without disrupting the team.
- Other: Remove Caleb’s inspection notes — Removing work harms the record the team needs.

### 21. One person is reassigned

**Control:** card

A technician moves to another job. Your brief is due this shift. One check remains unfinished.


### 22. One check remains unfinished after a technician leaves. Your brief is due this shift. What do you message Sora?

**Control:** chat

- Other: “I’ll mark the check complete to meet the deadline.” — A deadline does not make incomplete work complete.
- Other: “I’ll take over the check without supervision.” — The assignment and supervision still belong to the qualified team.
- Correct: “Can you reset priorities? I’ll list the open check and next owner.” — Sora can organize the work while your note keeps its unfinished status clear.

### 23. Facts reach the reviewer

**Control:** card

You send Sora the brief. It lists the rub mark, kit hold, and unfinished check for review.


### 24. Priya needs a hand

**Control:** card

Priya, a support mechanic on the next shift, asks how to record an unfinished check.


### 25. You have time to help

**Control:** card

Your brief is submitted. Priya’s handoff is due next. She needs the open check, status, and reviewer.


### 26. Priya needs help recording an unfinished check. You have time. Rank these responses from most to least supportive.

**Control:** rank

Correct ranking:
1. Record the status with Priya and notify Sora together
2. Share the handoff guide and remain available
3. Leave Priya to guess without support
4. Sign the unfinished check complete for Priya

### 27. Sora notices the difference

**Control:** card

“Clear notes help the next team. They show what still needs review and who to ask.”


### 28. First shift complete

**Control:** card

Your brief is delivered. Authorized staff assess the concerns and decide when the aircraft can fly again.


### 29. Your first airline shift

**Control:** review

You used current instructions, recorded concerns, checked part records, and supported the next shift.


## Score contract

Start at 50. Seven decisions award +6 or -6 once each. Four rapid items count as one decision; three correct pass that round. Practice changes neither score nor progress. Midpoint follows decision four at threshold 68. Final pass threshold 85 requires all seven decisions to pass: perfect 92; one wrong 80. Retry restarts the full shift. No future level is unlocked.
