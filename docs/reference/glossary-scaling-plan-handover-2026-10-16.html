Dreamari Glossary Games: Scaling Plan and Build Guide

Handover for Usman and his agents. 16 October 2026. The rules are Joshua's, from the "Dreamari Glossary Game Master Scaling Plan". The system that enforces them lives in the Dreamari prototype repo under src/components/glossary/, spec at docs/handoff/specs/glossary-system.md.

This document has three parts. The first half is the plan: the rules every new glossary game follows. The second is the build guide: the one file an agent writes, the checks that run on it, and the step list that turns "create a glossary game for X" into a playable game. The third is the image brief for Codex. Read the first part once. Use the second every time. Codex owns the third.

Status. The system is built and on main. Four generated industries exist beside Finance, each with the four playable levels the plan asks for: Software Engineering, Flight Operations, Nursing, Culinary Arts. The generated games are not on the Play hub yet; they play from the Glossary QA page (hamburger menu) until Chandu releases them. Division of labour: Codex generates images, Claude writes blueprints and builds games.

Part 1. The plan

1.1 Executive rules

These override anything else in the document.

1.2 Three glossary world architectures

ModeWhat it meansUse it whenDo not use it when
Continuous Story continuous_storyOne company, project, product or mission progresses across Levels 1 to 4. Later events build on earlier ones.Vocabulary has a natural cause-and-effect progression.You would have to invent awkward plot points just to introduce a term.
Scenario Thread scenario_threadThe student stays in the same professional world, but each lesson is a new situation: a new shift, case, customer, patient or problem.The workplace is consistent but the vocabulary does not need one continuous plot.One coherent project would genuinely improve understanding.
Practice World practice_worldShort, varied mini-situations. There is a visual world and Dreamy, but no obligation to keep a narrative.Vocabulary is best learned through rapid identification, comparison, classification, procedures, decisions or examples.A recurring situation would materially improve understanding.

A scenario thread is still a world. Nursing keeps the student inside one clinic, but nobody has to remember Patient A from Level 1 to understand Patient B in Level 3. That is far more scalable than turning every career into a television show.

1.4 How difficulty progresses

The learning progression stays the same across all 50 worlds even though the worlds change. A Level 4 question is not a longer Level 1 question. It requires better judgment. Math is one possible mechanic, never a required marker of difficulty.

LevelCognitive goalExample
Level 1Recognize and understand"Which term means money earned from sales?"
Level 2Apply"Maya puts $25,000 into the company. What does that money become?"
Level 3Compare, analyze, classifyInterpret relationships, consequences, workflows; tell two confusable words apart.
Level 4Decide, diagnose, distinguish"The company needs money but does not want to give up ownership. Which option fits?"
Power PlayIntegrateOne situation requiring all five terms together.

In the system each level has a focus: recognize, apply, analyze or decide. It defaults by level number (1, 2, 3, 4 and later) and can be overridden. The focus picks the recipe. The numbers in brackets are which of the five terms the question credits.

1.6 Story mode does not determine question type

A continuous story lesson still contains matching, multiple choice, sorting, drag and drop, sequencing, error detection, decisions and fill-in-the-blank, with calculations only when the team decides they are relevant. The story is the contextual spine: one context line, then the question. The story makes the information meaningful. It never becomes additional reading.

1.7 Artwork

Do not build a unique illustration for every term. 50 worlds times 4 levels times 5 terms is 1,000 terms; bespoke imagery for all of them is a production bottleneck and invites misleading visuals for abstract concepts. The team decides whether each term has an honest, relevant image. Candidates: sneaker, stethoscope, circuit breaker, wrench, aircraft engine, syringe, welding torch, hair clipper, CNC machine. Terms that do not need a standalone image: profit, liability, risk, margin, differential diagnosis, return, leverage, compliance, strategy.

ModeVisual strategy
Continuous storyVisualize the entity progressing; reuse the same company or product assets and change their state.
Scenario threadReuse a small library of environments, equipment, documents and tools.
Practice worldRely on interactions, Dreamy reactions, motion, clouds, buttons and success states.

In the system: Level 1 of every industry gets pictures for its five terms and one welcome illustration (decision of 16 October, section 3.4). Every later level teaches with words: words-only teach cards, initials in the mastery tokens, no pictures on match, sort or card backs. A flashcard never shows an icon in place of a picture.

1.8 Dreamy's role

Dreamy is part of the interaction system, not an illustration beside every piece of content. One screen, one Dreamy. Dreamy is the cloud itself: no arms, hands, legs or body. Dreamy is curious before a question, thinking during a hard one, concerned near a warning, excited after a correct answer, encouraging after a mistake, celebrating mastery, confident during the Power Play, surprised by an unexpected result. When term artwork is not appropriate, the game still comes from Dreamy's expressions, cloud movement, background animation, button reactions, correct-answer states, mastery animations, combos, unlocks, celebrations and transitions. A screen does not need a vocabulary illustration to feel like a game.

In the system: one actor per screen, twelve animated Drift poses and four Signal reactions mapped to question kinds and answer states, always facing the content, with the reward pluses on the main Dreamy when a student is right.

Final scaling principle (Joshua). The goal is not to make vocabulary flashcards look like a game. The goal is to make students repeatedly use professional language inside increasingly realistic situations, with the speed, clarity, feedback and satisfaction of a game. Story, images, math and specific mechanics are tools, not requirements. Across 50 worlds the system stays consistent in clarity, interaction, feedback, mastery and progression, while each profession determines the most authentic way to teach its language.

2.2 Create a glossary game for X (agent runbook)

  1. Look X up in the 50-world table. Take its mode and its career world. The industry slug is lowercase letters and dashes; it is the route (/play/glossary/<slug>).
  2. Write the blueprint at src/components/glossary/blueprints/<slug>.ts. Copy the one with the same mode: software.ts (continuous story, four levels), aviation.ts (scenario thread), culinary.ts (practice world). Fill in the shape in 2.3 following the copy rules in 2.4.
  3. Register it in GLOSSARY_INDUSTRIES in src/components/glossary/data.ts: <slug>: buildGlossaryCareer(X_GLOSSARY),
  4. Map its careers in CAREER_INDUSTRY (industries.ts) if the world has more than one glossary or the career's world cannot be inferred. Otherwise skip.
  5. Hub card: add to GLOSSARY_GAMES in src/components/play/games.ts if not listed. Key it to the example career's slug, or to the industry slug when no catalog career owns it. The card becomes playable by itself.
  6. Check:

node scripts/qa/dreamy-reactions.test.mjs

  7. Level 1 art (optional): only if the team decides the pictures clearly help. Prompt in 2.6; registration in 2.7.

2.4 Copy rules

  • Eighth grade or lower. Short words. One idea per sentence. Say each fact once.
  • No dashes of any kind in copy. Write two sentences instead.
  • No numbers unless the level sets math: true. Spell small numbers out ("thirty five thousand feet", "twelve hours") when they are part of the story rather than the math.
  • The definition never says its own word. The Which Word question reads the definition aloud and asks for the word.
  • The situation never says its word. It is the answer.
  • Every example says its word once, in a form that reads well with the word removed.
  • In a continuous story or scenario thread, every example names the anchor. In a practice world, most do not.
  • Cues are unique within a level and never longer than seven words.
  • Level titles 30 characters, goals 48 characters. The engine scales a long term on the teach card, wraps HUD labels to two lines, breaks long words in tiles and clips cards at their edges; it never scrolls.

Every one of the measurable rules above is checked by validateBlueprint at import time. A bad blueprint fails in development with a list of every problem, not in a student's hands.

2.5 What the student sees

  • Welcome: "Meet <anchor>. Learn <subject> by playing it." Dreamy alone, facing the copy (Finance's sneaker shop is Finance's own).
  • Teach cards: the five words, words only, each with its definition and example. Skippable.
  • Seven questions from the level's recipe, Dreamy reacting to each.
  • Review: Which Word for any term still short of mastery.
  • Power Play: one paragraph, five chips, five slots.
  • Complete: celebration; a continuous story shows the company value counting up, every other mode shows the milestone in words and "Level n of N complete".
  • Levels map: every level with its words, its goal and either the value it unlocks (continuous story) or its milestone.
