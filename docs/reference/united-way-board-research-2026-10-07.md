## 1. What United Way is, from our own research

**Scale and structure.** United Way Worldwide is a network of roughly 1,100 local United Ways in the US (about 1,800 worldwide). Programs are chosen, funded and run locally; Worldwide sets the impact framework and brokers national partnerships that locals opt into. "Youth Opportunity" is one of its named impact areas, with College & Career Readiness as a focus; Worldwide's own claim is that "87% of youth served gained employment" through mentorship, skill building, career coaching and real-world experience.

**How national programs actually reach students.** The model is a national partner plus a free product, activated locally:
- NFL + United Way + EVERFI, Character Playbook: a free digital course for middle schools, 1,000,000+ students, 12,000+ schools since 2016; local United Ways (Orange County with the Chargers) run the celebration events. This is the closest precedent for Dreamari: a national partner puts a platform in schools, the local United Way activates it.
- United Way Worldwide + Big Brothers Big Sisters: evidence-based one-to-one mentoring in the elementary and middle schools that feed the lowest-performing high schools; data shared with the school so the mentor is "part of the team" when a mentee skips school or fails a test. Communities: Winston-Salem, Fort Lauderdale, Austin, Louisville.
- MENTOR (the National Mentoring Partnership): local United Ways lead MENTOR affiliates (United Way of the National Capital Area with Deloitte), which set program standards for the 5,000-program mentoring field.

**Mentorship is real and at scale, and it is high school.**
- United Way of Palm Beach County's Mentor Center runs a network of 60 mentor programs and 6,500 matches a year; outcomes reported: 100% graduated on time, 98% increased college and career preparation skills, 90% satisfactory attendance, 88% developed life skills.
- Orange County United Way's e-Mentorship: high school seniors from low income communities, one-to-one professional mentors plus a six-month workshop series (financial aid, life after high school, mental health, work-life balance). Class of 2021: 285 students, 100% graduated on time, 90% confident about college. Being brought back for the Class of 2027.
- United Way of Salt Lake's Promise Student Advocates: 533 high school students with adult mentors as of December 2025.
- United Way of the Midlands' Young Men United: 100% matched with an adult mentor, paid eight-week internships, job shadows, laptops.
So the answer to "do they offer mentorship" is yes, in many chapters, mostly high school seniors, mostly virtual or monthly, run through partner nonprofits and corporate volunteers. That belongs in our Mentorship tab.

**Career exposure is run as events.** Albuquerque (United Ways with the district's CTE office): eighth-grade career exploration days with employers. Miami-Dade Career Connections (United Way, the Beacon Council, the school district). United Way Bay Area's OnTrack Career Expo: hands-on exhibits plus adult mentors "with similar lived experiences". Southwest Virginia's Ignite internships: 79 companies across 19 counties.

**Volunteers and hours are the currency.** United Way serves 115,000 workplace campaigns reaching 47.5 million employees (with Salesforce Philanthropy Cloud). Locals run volunteer platforms such as INVOLVE and "My Way | Volunteer", both on Galaxy Digital's Get Connected, whose selling point is hour tracking and "economic impact" reports instead of spreadsheets. Corporate partners are recognised by money and hours (Caterpillar: $15.2M in the 2025 campaign, named a 2025 Leading Corporate Partner; the Caterpillar Foundation's $250K Pathways to Work grant in Dallas; Greater Cincinnati's $2.3M youth and workforce readiness investments; Atlanta's CareerReady ATL).

**How they measure.** Since 2015 Worldwide runs the Global Results Framework (GRF): a common set of reach and outcome indicators per impact area, reported annually by locals in the Global Results Snapshot (on Clear Impact Scorecard). Youth Success indicators include youth receiving job skills training, soft skills gained, school attendance, on-time graduation, and postsecondary employment or credential. Locals may report as little as one measure, so adoption is uneven. Charlotte's Collective Impact work (16 agencies, common measures) found about 90% of at-risk students with three or more years in United Way programs graduated, tracking attendance, suspensions and academics.

**Gaps we found.** No network-wide student-facing platform. Mentoring, career exposure and reporting each live in different tools (Galaxy Digital for hours, Clear Impact for outcomes, EVERFI for courses, email and FaceTime for mentors). Program pages publish outcomes by hand, sometimes with placeholder zeros. The e-Mentorship program went dormant and is being rebuilt.

## 2. How Dreamari and Connect work, and why that fits

Dreamari's thesis: 376 students per counselor, so the app itself has to make students (and the adults around them) better at knowing careers. The student journey is Build → Match → Top 3 → Explore → Play → Connect → Opportunities → My Plan, with the Career Report and the Dream Score as the throughline.

Connect's goal, from the locked spec: students ask verified professionals and public answers help many students at once; communities are industry boards with pros and company marks; event boards unlock with a code after a real event (the Attendee role); People is follow-a-professional; Mentorship is the Coach pattern (one to one, year plan, message dock, hours), college stage only today because high school students have no private messaging. Volunteers get routed questions, time-boxed requests, a private My Impact with metric tiles, a 30-day area chart and rings; Partners get company-level impact for recognition and sponsorship reporting. Safety is built in: verified pros, moderated questions, Report with reasons, contact details blocked.

Put the two side by side and every United Way activity already has a home:

| United Way does | Dreamari surface that already exists |
| --- | --- |
| Career speakers, site visits, employer visits, career expos | Connect Events (ticket, code, event board with Questions, Posts, Insights, Resources, People) and Opportunities |
| Internships, job shadows, four-week workplace placements | Opportunities cards with Add to My Plan, Save, status and dates (the AT&T board's version) |
| Public Q&A with professionals | Community board Ask, routed to verified volunteers |
| One-to-one mentoring with a workshop series | Mentorship tab (Coach pattern: match, year plan, message dock, hours), extended to high school for United Way programs |
| College prep (financial aid, applications, scholarships) | My Plan tasks, the Career Report, Explore Schools |
| Corporate volunteers logging hours | Volunteer role: Today requests, routed questions, hours by month, impact summary |
| Chapter and funder reporting (GRF) | Partner role: impact view, now in GRF vocabulary |

## 3. Recommendation: one United Way presence, three layers, built from parts we have

Not "board or mentorship". Both, plus the reporting layer, because United Way is three audiences at once and our Connect roles already map onto them.

**Layer 1, students: the United Way board, national with local activation.**
A Connect community board, "United Way · Student Success", built on the AT&T v2.0 structure (reach first, local center optional). Tabs: Home · Programs · Ask · Opportunities · Events · People. A chapter picker in the banner (national content for everyone; a chapter adds its programs, events, employers and people). Programs are cards: who it is for, when, what you get, one tap for "I'm interested". Program cards that are mentoring open the mentorship program (Layer 2). Events use our ticket-and-code board, so a student who went to a career expo keeps the conversation going afterwards, which is exactly the gap United Way's events have today.

**Layer 2, mentorship: United Way programs inside the Mentorship tab.**
The Coach pattern generalised to "Mentorship programs": each United Way program (e-Mentorship, Promise Student Advocates, Young Men United, the Mentor Center's network) is a program with its own cadence, year plan, workshop series and match. Two changes to what Coach has: high school stage allowed (these programs serve seniors), and workshops as dated program events the mentee and mentor both see. The message dock stays private and moderated; hours and meetings roll up per program. This is the part United Way has researched for years and not bought (Coach said the same in September).

**Layer 3, volunteers and the chapter: hours and GRF reporting out of the box.**
Volunteers: the existing Volunteer role. Today opens with "Since you were last here", three time-boxed requests (answer a question, review a résumé, speak at an expo), Impact with hours by month against the chapter's goal, and an export in the shape Galaxy Digital and Salesforce expect (hours, date, activity, student count without names). Chapter and Worldwide: the Partner role, with the impact view speaking GRF: youth reached, youth receiving job skills training, soft skills gained, mentoring matches and meetings, internships and job shadows, on-time graduation and postsecondary plan where the school shares it, plus Dreamari engagement (careers explored and saved, simulations played, questions answered). A yearly Impact Summary card (the Spotify Wrapped pattern Connect already has) for each chapter.

**Why this is the best outcome for each party**
- Students: programs, mentors, events and internships from their local United Way appear inside the app they already use for careers, with the same safety rules, instead of a flyer and an email thread. A student in a chapter with no programs still gets the national board.
- Volunteers: short, routed asks that fit a lunch break, one place to answer and mentor, hours that count toward their employer's United Way campaign.
- United Way: the platform they do not have, a program toolkit a chapter can switch on without building anything, and GRF-ready numbers for the Global Results Snapshot without spreadsheets. Dormant programs like e-Mentorship can restart on day one.
- Dreamari: distribution through a network of 1,100 locals and their corporate partners, and a reusable partner kit (board + mentorship program + impact) that already serves AT&T, Coach and Junior Achievement.

**Why not mentorship only, and why not board only**
Mentorship only leaves four of six researched program types (expos, speakers, placements, college prep) with no home, and most chapters start there. Board only ignores the thing United Way runs at the largest scale (6,500 matches a year in one county) and that Chandu named explicitly. The three layers reuse what exists; the net new work is the chapter picker, the Programs tab, the high school mentorship variant with workshops, and the GRF impact view.

## 4. Analytics, using the chart system we already have

Every chart below is an existing component. No new chart styles.

| View | What it shows | Component (file) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter Impact, top row | Youth reached, Active this month, Volunteer hours, Matches, each with a 30-day delta | `MetricTile` (connect/viz.tsx) |

| Top careers students saved in this chapter | Ranked with slim gradient bars, Explore poster imagery beside | `RankedBars` / `TopTen` (counselor/v4/CareerCollegeInsights.tsx) |
| GRF indicators | One row per indicator with the value, a bar to the chapter's goal, a benchmark tick for last year | `BarRow` with `tick` (counselor/v4/MyImpact.tsx) |
| Volunteer Impact | Hours by month, with the chapter goal line | mentorship `BarChart` with `compare`, `Sparkline` in tiles |
| Mentorship program | Meetings per pair, pairs on cadence, workshop attendance | mentorship `charts.tsx` as the Coach program uses them |
| Impact Summary | The year's four numbers on one shareable card | the Connect volunteer Impact Summary card |

Design budget as always: blue plus status colours, one verdict line per block, gradient bars, no card tints, 8th-grade copy.

## 5. Questions for tonight

**Partnership scope**
1. Who is the partner: United Way Worldwide, a regional network, or specific local United Ways? Who signs, and who activates locally?
2. Is the model "national program, free to schools, local United Ways activate" (the Character Playbook shape), or chapter by chapter?
3. Which chapters would go first, and what do they run today: mentoring, career expos, internships, college prep?
4. What is the timeline: pilot in one chapter this school year, or a network launch?

**Students and programs**
5. Which grades and which students: all high school, seniors only, specific schools, income-qualified?
6. For mentoring programs: cadence, duration, virtual or in person, how matches are made today, and what the workshop series covers.
7. Which programs are dormant and waiting for a platform (e-Mentorship for the Class of 2027 is the example)?
8. How do students find out about programs today, and where do they sign up?

**Volunteers and safety**
9. Who are the volunteers: corporate employees from campaign partners, community volunteers, retirees? Which companies?
10. What screening do mentors go through today (background checks, training), and who holds that record?
11. Do volunteers log hours in INVOLVE, Galaxy Digital, Salesforce Philanthropy Cloud or something else? Would an hours export from Dreamari count?
12. What are the rules for adult-to-minor contact: can a mentor message a student one to one, and under whose supervision?

**Data and reporting**
13. Which Global Results Framework indicators does the chapter report each year, and in what format (Clear Impact Scorecard)?
14. Which school data can be shared with us: attendance, on-time graduation, postsecondary plan? Under what agreement?
15. What do corporate partners want to see for recognition: hours, students served, economic value of hours?
16. What would the chapter show its board or funders that it cannot show today?

**Brand and launch**
17. Who owns the United Way mark decisions for a co-branded board, and are there chapter lockups we must use?
18. Who writes program copy and who approves it before students see it?
19. Can we talk to one program lead and one volunteer before design lock?
20. What would make them say "wow" at the first demo?
