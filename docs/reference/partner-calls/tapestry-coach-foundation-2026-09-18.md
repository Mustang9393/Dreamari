# Tapestry / Coach Foundation call (Coach Dream Program), Sept 2026

Two people from the Coach One Nation / Tapestry Social Impact team (3-person
team: two of them plus an executive director). One leads the Dream Program
mentorship; the other is the volunteering engagement lead for the Coach
Foundation. Sister teams: Tapestry Foundation (leather supply chain,
environment) and Kate Spade Social Impact (women and girls' mental health).

## Their program, as described

- US: 5th year. 450 scholars this year (250 before), each matched 1:1 with an
  employee mentor. Runs October to April, Dream Day in June. Cohort supported
  across all 4 years of college, mentor assigned freshman year.
- Mentors: about 50% corporate, 50% retail field (store managers with up to
  30 years at Coach). Recruitment cycle each autumn, mentor orientation on
  do's and don'ts with young people.
- Required 3 to 4 meetings per academic year; most pairs meet monthly or
  more. In person or virtual.
- Year 2 rematching: mentee and mentor each say "same again" or "new".
  Described as a "puzzle piece phase" done by hand.
- UK: 35 students, monthly, stricter cadence set by the nonprofit partner.
  Japan: about 33, April to September, extra strict, monthly.
  China: 1,000 students, 1-year scholarship, no 1:1 matching (legal reasons
  and not enough volunteers); quarterly regional mentorship events instead.
- Students sourced through nonprofit partners; all are scholarship recipients.
- Communication today: mentee and mentor pick their own channel. Mostly
  email, some text, FaceTime, in person. "It is my dream to track all of
  this."
- Metrics they roll up: mentorship hours per employee; Tapestry-wide goal of
  500,000 volunteer hours by 2030; Coach Foundation goal of 10,000
  scholarships by 2030. Their definition of success for a student: the least
  amount of debt at graduation.
- Employee feedback: "the best part of working at Coach" is being a mentor.
  Their own pain: getting brand and global teams to collaborate, and getting
  the business to understand why social impact work exists.

## What they said about Dreamari

Validated
- "Incredible platform. The design is really, really beautiful."
- "I love how clear the data is laid out. A lot of platforms are wonky, this
  is so clear." (the partner and volunteer dashboards)
- Home, Explore and Play are "very unique, not something I have seen on
  mentorship platforms."
- Connect answers "a lot of the solutions we have been looking for" to reach
  students outside Dream Day. Public Q&A means one answer helps many
  students, which they called the best part of Dream Day too.
- They have researched mentorship platforms for a year and have not bought
  one. Students have made unofficial group chats, which worries them.

Questions and gaps (verbatim intent)
1. Safety and moderation: how is it kept safe?
2. Verification: when a volunteer says "product designer at X", is that
   checked?
3. Private messaging between a volunteer and a student, to set meetings up.
   Public Q&A alone does not cover a 1:1 mentorship.
4. A virtual call inside the app.
5. Activity data without reading the conversations: "Julie messaged this
   person 15 times this month", converted into volunteer hours.
6. Impact on college experience and career readiness, over four years, not
   just engagement counts.
7. A stated platform goal, e.g. "this many students engaged by 2030".
8. Debt comparison between schools (NYU vs Rutgers), since least debt is
   their success metric.
9. Trade school pathways.
10. "Baking in additional metrics would widen who you can market to."
