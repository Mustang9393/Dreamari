DREAMARI MASTER CAREER SIMULATION IMAGE STANDARD
Create a single cohesive cinematic 2D anime illustration for a Dreamari career simulation. The character, environment, furniture, props, lighting, shadows, and background people must feel like they were illustrated together as one unified scene, not a character pasted over a separate background.
CAREER: [CAREER]
LEVEL / ROLE: [LEVEL + ROLE]
SCENE: [WHAT IS HAPPENING]
LOCATION: [WORKPLACE / ENVIRONMENT]
TIME OF DAY: [TIME]
CHARACTERS: [CHARACTER DETAILS + REFERENCE IMAGES]

1. Overall visual direction
Use a premium, polished cinematic Japanese 2D television-anime look with clean two-tone cel shading, strong silhouettes, thin controlled contour lines, expressive anime facial construction, and a simplified hand-designed background.
The image should feel comparable in production quality to a high-end television anime, but it must remain its own Dreamari visual identity. Do not imitate another franchise's characters, costumes, colors, or world.
Design the frame as an anime production, not a realistically rendered illustration with anime eyes added afterward.
Use clear shapes first, selective detail second. Keep the character and background at a compatible level of simplification.
Avoid the default AI-generated look:
no hyper-detailed textures everywhere
no excessive micro-detail
no photorealistic or painterly skin rendering
no western comic-book facial modeling
no heavy black comic contours, crosshatching, stippling, or inked cheek planes
no 3D-render appearance
no plastic skin
no dense soft gradients on faces or clothing
no excessive sharpening
no over-rendered hair

2. Background simplification
The environment should use simplified anime background design:
clean architectural shapes
large readable color blocks
a small number of meaningful details
simplified furniture and equipment
limited visual clutter
clear foreground, midground, and background
Use broad planes for walls, counters, floors, cabinets, windows, and major equipment. Describe their shape and function without rendering every seam, surface grain, screw, button, or cable.
Do not fill every surface with objects.
Only include equipment that helps explain the career or current scene.
For offices, for example, use a small number of monitors, desks, chairs, plants, documents, or work tools rather than filling the entire frame with screens.
For clinical spaces, retain the essential bed, rail, headwall, monitor, IV, or relevant care equipment without inventing extra machines or tubing.
Keep distant trees, buildings, clouds, and plants as grouped shapes. Do not make the view outside more detailed than the main character.
Background people should be simplified secondary figures with readable silhouettes, minimal faces, broad hair shapes, and a few clothing folds. They should support a functioning workplace without competing with the main characters.

3. Color standard
Keep the colors vibrant but controlled.
Use:
richer blues
clean sky colors
canonical, healthy skin tones
selective warm accents
deep navy and charcoal
genuinely dark black points
Use a clear base color and a restrained shadow color on characters. Broad lighting transitions may appear in the environment, but they must not become dense gradients on every surface.
Dark areas should remain dark enough to make the image feel premium and cinematic.
Do not wash the whole image in orange, yellow, or artificial golden-hour lighting.
The environment may have warm sunlight, but blues, blacks, whites, skin tones, and neutral surfaces must retain their own color.
Do not lighten, darken, or recolor an existing character's canonical skin tone to match a style reference.

4. Lighting
Use one believable dominant light source appropriate for the time of day.
Characters and the environment must respond to that same light source.
Include:
consistent shadow direction
one broad, readable cel-shadow plane on skin where appropriate
restrained cel-shadow shapes on clothing
subtle contact shadows
believable, limited environmental bounce light
Use shadow shape and value to explain form. Do not model faces with many small cheek, forehead, nose, neck, or under-eye patches.
Avoid:
character lighting that does not match the room
glowing outlines around characters
artificial halos
excessive rim lighting
blown-out highlights
repeated glare on every surface
multiple suns or contradictory light directions

5. Reflections and glare
Surfaces should look premium but not excessively glossy.
Floors and tables may have a subtle satin or polished finish, but they should not behave like mirrors.
Use:
soft, muted reflections
restrained highlights
subtle floor contact
broad, quiet material tones
Avoid:
giant light streaks across floors
bright glare patches on tables
mirror-like marble
reflections of every object
unnecessary specular highlights
Reflections exist primarily to ground the characters, not to become part of the composition.

6. Character integration
The character must physically belong in the environment.
Match:
perspective
scale
lighting
color temperature
shadow direction
line weight
rendering detail
At least one environmental element may overlap part of the character when compositionally natural, such as a desk edge, glass partition, chair, doorway, equipment, hospital bed, aircraft component, or workstation.
Characters should cast believable contact shadows where appropriate.
A hand holding a tool must connect naturally to the arm and body. A person leaning on a counter, sitting in a bed, or handing something over must have believable physical contact with that environment.
Never create the appearance of a clean character sprite simply placed over a backdrop.

7. Character rendering
Main characters should use:
clean Japanese 2D anime facial construction
expressive, clearly designed anime eyes that retain adult proportions
simplified noses and mouths
smooth base skin color with one broad cel-shadow plane where appropriate
thin controlled facial lines
large, intentional hair masses
a small number of purposeful clothing folds
readable expressions
Avoid western comic-book jaw and cheek modeling, heavy brows outlined in black, clusters of forehead creases, crosshatched wrinkles, painterly facial detail, or glossy skin.
Keep adults visibly adult through facial structure, proportions, posture, and canonical features. Do not make mature characters look like teenagers by enlarging the eyes or shrinking the face excessively.
Hair is especially important: preserve the hairstyle's silhouette while rebuilding its rendering from broad solid shapes.
For most hairstyles, start with approximately five to eight major masses, with very few interior separation curves. Treat this as a simplification guide, not a reason to change the canonical hairstyle.
For short textured or curly hair, use a small number of broad scalloped silhouette clusters, approximately eight to ten where suitable. Retain the character's natural hair texture without drawing individual curls, curls inside curls, spring-like ringlets, or dozens of tiny highlight marks.
For buns and updos, use a few overlapping solid masses. Do not fill the bun with concentric curling lines.
For braids, use a limited sequence of broad interlocking shapes rather than drawing every strand within each segment.
Use one base hair color and, where necessary, one broad shadow or restrained cel accent. Keep gray streaks as a few deliberate shapes.
Allow only a few purposeful framing locks or flyaways. Do not add fine strands simply to demonstrate detail.
Characters should generally be visually appealing and professionally designed without being sexualized.

8. Character identity consistency
When a character already exists, preserve their canonical:
face silhouette and identifying features
skin tone
age
hairstyle silhouette
hair color
eye color
build
wardrobe
accessories
approximate height
Use the supplied character reference image as the identity source.
A style reference controls linework, shading, and simplification only. It must not replace the character's face, ethnicity, clothing, age, or environment.
Clearly distinguish the inputs:
character reference: identity only
location reference: architecture and layout only
approved Dreamari anime frame: style and rendering only
existing scene being edited: identity, action, composition, and continuity
Do not copy a reference character or reference room into an unrelated scene.
Do not redesign an existing character between scenes. Preserve an approved face and hair design across welcoming, concerned, proud, working, and tired expressions.

9. Cast diversity across careers
Do not recycle the same face, body type, ethnicity, hairstyle, or overall character design across unrelated Dreamari career simulations.
Each career should have its own identifiable cast.
When creating a new character, deliberately vary:
ethnicity
facial structure
age
hair
body build
height
clothing
posture
visual personality
Two characters with different names should never appear to be the same generated person.
Simplifying hair must not erase culturally or ethnically meaningful texture. Simplifying a face must not erase age or identifying features.

10. Pose and storytelling
The scene must communicate what is happening in the simulation.
Characters should be:
working
reacting
walking
inspecting
speaking
observing
handing something over
using equipment
interacting with coworkers, clients, patients, machinery, documents, or the environment
Avoid making every character stand directly toward the camera like a portrait.
Use a clear action and a clear point of attention. Do not invent unrelated activities or make a character operate several tools at once unless the scene explicitly requires it.
Friendly characters may smile when appropriate because Dreamari should feel aspirational and exciting.
Expressions should still fit the story moment. A concerned reaction, anxious patient, serious inspection, or urgent incident must remain recognizable after the style is simplified.
Check anatomy and object handling: the correct number of arms, hands, fingers, and tools; plausible grip; no extra hand created to explain a gesture.

11. Career authenticity
Every image must visually belong to the specific career.
Include only the tools, spaces, uniforms, equipment, workplace conditions, and professional relationships that make sense for that role and career level.
Preserve scene-specific professional details such as protective clothing, identification, clinical equipment, inspection tools, or safety gear when required by the supplied scene.
Do not invent a procedure, tool use, symptom, uniform, or workplace condition simply to make the frame more dramatic.
The student should feel:
“This is what it might actually feel like to work here.”

12. Scene continuity
Recurring environments must remain recognizable across the simulation.
Preserve:
room architecture
workstation layout
major furniture
equipment
windows and their orientation
important landmarks
view outside the windows
overall color palette
Use an approved scene or location anchor for recurring spaces rather than inventing the room again from a text description.
Time-of-day variants should look like the same place at a different time, not a newly invented environment.
When a person appears in successive states, preserve the same identity, hairstyle design, clothing, camera relationship, and relevant equipment. Change only the action, expression, condition, or lighting required by the story.

13. Time-of-day treatment
Match the sky and lighting to the story.
Day: clean blue sky, neutral daylight, moderate contrast.
Afternoon: slightly warmer sunlight while the sky remains clearly blue.
Early sunset: blue upper sky with restrained orange/pink near the horizon.
Late sunset: warmer sky and deeper interior shadows, used selectively for high-aura or important story moments.
Night: cool dark blues with restrained practical lighting.
Do not use dramatic sunset lighting simply because it looks attractive. It must correspond to the story.
Keep the view, room geometry, and cast recognizable across time variants. Do not place a bright daytime skyline behind a night scene or invent a second sun to create extra highlights.

14. Composition for Dreamari UI
Generate only the illustration.
Do not generate:
buttons
dialogue boxes
progress bars
Reputation meters
names
captions
interface icons
game text
The Dreamari UI will be placed separately.
Use the actual target game screen or overlay reference when available. Plan for its top HUD, dialogue card, answer panel, and any career instrument before placing the important visual information.
Leave visually quiet negative space wherever the planned interface card will appear. A quiet bottom third is a useful starting point, but some instrument panels extend higher; use the actual overlay rather than assuming every slide has the same safe area.
Important faces, gestures, hands, tools, and story information must remain visible after the UI is added. Do not put a medication packet, scanner head, handover notebook, or other essential action behind the dialogue card.
Keep faces below the top HUD and above the dialogue area. As a starting guide, faces often belong within approximately the upper 8–45% of the frame; the actual UI and camera composition take priority.
For mobile, identify the main story subject and keep the essential face and action inside the planned portrait crop. Do not scatter essential faces across both edges of a wide frame.
If a two-person scene or a wide action cannot remain legible in both crops, create a separately composed portrait version when requested. Do not solve the problem by making characters or props tiny, cutting off important faces, or changing the scene's meaning.
Verify the illustration inside the real desktop and phone UI before accepting it, not only as a standalone image.

15. Screens and written information
Keep the quantity of computer monitors and digital displays low.
If a display is necessary, use simple believable visual information such as:
a basic chart
a few bars
a simplified spreadsheet structure
simple diagnostic information
Use a few clean shapes or traces rather than dense generated screen content. Dynamic readings and important instructions belong in the native game UI.
Do not generate dense fake dashboards, gibberish text, dozens of charts, screens filled with illegible information, names, patient identifiers, or invented numerical readings.
Documents, badges, notebooks, and labels should remain blank or use sparse non-readable structural marks. If scanning a barcode is essential to the action, use a simple barcode pattern without invented words or numbers.

16. Final quality check
Before accepting the image, verify:
STYLE: Does the entire frame look like one coherent Japanese 2D anime production rather than a western comic, painting, or 3D render?
CHARACTER: Does the person match their canonical identity, age, ethnicity, proportions, wardrobe, and accessories?
BACKGROUND: Is it intentionally simplified into readable architecture and color blocks rather than over-generated texture?
LIGHTING: Are character and room lit by the same believable source with consistent shadow direction?
BLACK POINTS: Are navy, charcoal, and black areas deep enough to feel premium?
COLOR: Is the image vibrant without becoming oversaturated or washing every surface in warm light?
GLARE: Are reflections and highlights restrained?
CLUTTER: Are there only as many screens, devices, props, and people as the story needs?
GROUNDING: Does the character physically feel inside the environment, with plausible contact and object handling?
HAIR: Is the hairstyle built from a few broad solid masses with minimal interior strokes, without losing canonical texture or silhouette?
FACE: Are eyes, nose, mouth, and shadows clean and expressive without comic-book modeling, excessive creases, or childlike de-aging?
STORY: Can someone understand the action and emotional state before reading the dialogue?
CONTINUITY: Do recurring characters, rooms, windows, equipment, and time-of-day variants remain consistent?
UI: Are the essential face, gesture, and tools still visible in the real desktop and phone crops with the interface overlaid?
If any of these fail, revise only the failing element while preserving the approved identity, scene, composition, and other successful details. Re-check the full frame after the revision so a simplification does not introduce extra hands, missing props, or continuity drift.
Highest-priority instruction: Favor intentional simplification over additional detail. Dreamari images should feel designed, composed, and art-directed, not like the generator tried to demonstrate how much detail it could create.
